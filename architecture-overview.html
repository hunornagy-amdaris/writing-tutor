01 · The product in one minute

An AI essay tutor that scores, explains, and coaches

The Writing Tutor is a web application that helps English-language learners improve their essay writing. A student is given a writing task, drafts an essay, and receives a structured, sentence-by-sentence analysis. The app then guides the student through corrections — in text or via a live voice tutor — and lets them resubmit to see their score improve.

The experience is organised as a clear four-step journey. The student always knows where they are, what they just did, and what comes next.

1 Brainstorm Plan & explore

Before writing, the student talks through ideas with an AI brainstorming partner — by typing or by voice — to clarify their stance and collect supporting points for the prompt.

2 Write Draft

The student writes their essay in a focused editor. An optional AI writing coach is available on the side to answer questions or unblock the student without writing the essay for them.

3 Review Learn & fix

The essay is broken down sentence by sentence. Each sentence is shown alongside detected grammar issues, awkward phrasing, and suggested corrections. A voice tutor or a short quiz helps the student understand why each fix matters, not just what to change.

4 Score Measure progress

The essay is scored against established writing criteria — content, grammar, vocabulary, cohesion, and more. After applying their fixes, the student can resubmit and see the side-by-side improvement in their score.

02 · How the system is put together

Three layers, one experience

The product is built from three layers that work together. Each layer has one clear job, which keeps the system reliable and makes it straightforward to evolve over time.

The student only ever talks to the Writing Tutor App. The app talks to the AI services on the student's behalf, so credentials and prompts stay safely on the server.

The three layers, explained

Front of house
The interface

What the student sees and interacts with in their browser: the writing editor, the sentence-by-sentence review, the live voice tutor, and the scoring screen. Designed to feel calm and guided rather than busy.

The kitchen
The application server

Receives the student's essay, decides which AI services to call, validates every response, and assembles the final result. This is also the only place that holds API keys — the browser never sees them.

The specialists
External AI services

Three specialised providers handle the parts that need machine intelligence: writing analysis, voice conversation, and impartial language scoring. The app coordinates them.

Who does what among the AI providers

OpenAI
The analyst & coach

Reads the essay, identifies grammar errors and awkward phrasing, generates explanations, runs the brainstorming and writing-help chats, and creates short practice quizzes.

ElevenLabs
The voice tutor

Powers the natural-sounding spoken conversations in both the Brainstorm step and the sentence-by-sentence Review step, so the student can practise out loud.

Hugging Face
The impartial examiner

Provides two specialised language models that give a second, neutral opinion: one rates the writing across six dimensions, the other flags unnatural word choices.

03 · Why the result can be trusted

Quality, safety, and the second opinion

Because the app makes judgements about a student's writing, every score and every correction needs to be defensible. Several design choices exist specifically to keep results trustworthy and consistent.

Every essay is reviewed by two independent systems: a generative model that explains what to fix, and a specialised scoring model that gives a stable, repeatable mark. The two are then combined into the score the student sees.
  • Cross-checked scoring The five language sub-scores (cohesion, syntax, vocabulary, phraseology, grammar, conventions) come from a specialised scoring model rather than from the generative AI alone. This makes scores stable: the same essay submitted twice receives the same mark.
  • Sentence-level accountability Feedback is anchored to individual sentences, not just paragraphs. The student can see exactly which sentence each suggestion refers to and decide whether to accept it.
  • Validated AI responses Every reply from an AI service is checked against a strict schema before it reaches the student. Anything malformed is rejected on the server instead of being shown as a broken screen.
  • Graceful fallbacks If the specialised scoring model is unavailable, the app falls back to the generative model and clearly marks the source of the score. The student is never blocked.
  • Credentials stay on the server API keys for the AI providers are held only on the application server. The student's browser never holds a key and cannot be used to run up costs or abuse the providers.
  • No essay storage by default The app is stateless with respect to student work — essays live in the current browser session and are passed to AI providers only for the duration of a single request.

04 · Foundations & what comes next

Built on modern, well-supported foundations

The application uses widely adopted, actively maintained technology with strong ecosystems and developer availability. This protects the product against being stuck with niche or aging tools.

The web platform

The app is built on Next.js and React — the same foundation used by major publishers and SaaS products. It loads quickly, works on phones and laptops, and is easy to update.

How it is hosted

Deployed on Vercel, a managed hosting platform that scales automatically with traffic. No server administration is required, and updates roll out in minutes whenever code is published.

Design system

The visual language uses a consistent token-based design system. This means colours, spacing, and typography are defined in one place and applied uniformly, which keeps the product looking polished.

Operations

AI provider costs are usage-based and fully observable. The app logs each analysis run with a unique ID, so any reported issue can be traced end-to-end without re-running the student's essay.

Where the architecture is heading

The current shape supports the immediate product goal: a single student, a single essay, a complete coaching loop. A few natural extensions are already supported by the foundations:

  • Student accounts and history Adding sign-in and persistent essay history is a straightforward addition that does not change the core flow.
  • Teacher and classroom views The same scoring and review pipeline can feed dashboards for teachers tracking class progress over time.
  • Additional languages and task types The prompt-driven design means new writing tasks (letters, reports, academic essays) and additional target languages can be added incrementally.
  • Swappable AI providers Each AI service sits behind a clear boundary, so any one provider can be replaced or supplemented without rewriting the rest of the system.

06 · The analyze pipeline

POST /api/analyze — end-to-end

The analyze route is the central piece of the system: it composes scores from two independent models and enriches every sentence with masked-LM phraseology data. The same handler serves both the initial submission and later resubmissions.

01
Parse JSON body; reject non-JSON with 400.
02
Validate against requestSchema = { text: 1–10 000, prompt: 1–2 000 }. Extra fields are stripped.
03
Mint a request ID via wtRunId(); log essay fingerprint (length + first/last chars) for traceability.
04
Fan out in parallel via Promise.all: openai.chat.completions.create with response_format: { type: 'json_object' } using buildAnalyzerPrompt, and scoreKevSun(text) against the HF endpoint.
05
KevSun postprocess: raw regression logits → 2.25 · x − 1.25, clamp to [1, 5], snap to 0.5. Returns null if endpoint URL is unset, response is non-200, response shape is wrong, or values are softmaxed (sum ≈ 1).
06
Strip code fences from the OpenAI message, JSON.parse, then validate against analysisResultSchema (Zod). Failure → 500.
07
Per-sentence enrichment (serial across sentences, parallel within): for each sentence call checkPhraseology(original, grammarTokens). Each candidate word becomes a masked sentence sent to roberta-base with top_k=100.
08
Flag rule: a word is flagged when its rank in the MLM distribution at that position is > 80, or it doesn't appear in the top 100. Stop-words and tokens already covered by a grammar edit are skipped.
09
Phraseology sentence score = max(1, 5 − flags · 0.5), snapped to 0.5.
10
Score composition (when KevSun is present): five linguistic dims come from KevSun, content from the LLM, then PTE recomposition.
11
Overall = round05((content + cohesion + grammar + (syntax+phraseology)/2 + vocabulary + conventions) / 6).
12
Respond with the validated payload plus a __debug envelope containing the request ID, scoring source, raw KevSun, LLM scores, computed scores, and phraseology flag totals.
UI overall (PTE 10–90 scale)
// src/modules/writing-flow/lib/score-mapping.ts
const toPteOverall = (x1to5: number): number =>
  clamp(10 + Math.round(((x1to5 − 1) / 4) * 80), 10, 90);

08 · Data model

The contracts crossing every boundary

Every request and every response is validated with Zod on the server, and the analyze response is validated again on the client before it enters the store. The shapes below are the authoritative contract.

Analysis result — the analyze response
type AnalysisResult = {
  scores: {
    cohesion: number; syntax: number; vocabulary: number;
    phraseology: number; grammar: number; conventions: number;
    content: number; overall: number;          // 1.0–5.0, step 0.5
  };
  sentences: AnalyzedSentence[];
  paragraphs?: { label: 'Introduction' | 'Conclusion' | `Body ${number}`;
                 sentenceIndices: number[] }[];
  raw_kevsun?: RawKevSun;                // raw model logits (debug/anchor)
  kevsun_anchor?: { min: number; max: number };
};

type AnalyzedSentence = {
  original: string;
  corrected: string;
  has_errors: boolean;
  grammar_edits: { type: 'replace'|'delete'|'insert';
                    token: string; correction?: string }[];
  phraseology_flags: { token: string; rank: number;
                       suggestions: string[] }[];
  phraseology_score?: number;            // 1.0–5.0, server-computed
};
Zustand store — useFlowStore (single store, all UI state)
type FlowState = {
  step: FlowStep; maxStep: FlowStep;          // nav state, forward-only
  prompt: string; essay: string;
  brainstorm: { mode: 'type'|'voice'; messages: ChatMessage[]; isVoiceActive: boolean };
  analysis: AnalysisResult | null;             // current view
  initialAnalysis: AnalysisResult | null;       // preserved for comparison

Security boundaries
  • Server-only secrets. OPENAI_API_KEY and HUGGINGFACE_API_KEY are declared under server: in the env schema, so any client import would be a build-time error. The OpenAI SDK and HF fetch calls live in 'server-only' modules.
  • Zod-validated in and out. Every POST body is validated server-side (limits: text ≤ 10 000, prompt ≤ 2 000, messages ≤ 50); analyze responses are revalidated client-side against analysisResultSchema before they enter the store.
  • ElevenLabs key never reaches the browser. Voice tutors use the ElevenLabs public-agent flow with dynamic variables; no ELEVENLABS_API_KEY exists in the app.
Observability
  • Per-run identifiers. Every analyze call mints wtRunId() and threads it through logs and the __debug envelope returned to the client, so a single submission is traceable end-to-end.
  • Scoring source surfaced. Each run is tagged kevsun, llm-fallback, or unknown; the tag is shown on the Score screen and stored in runLog together with an essay fingerprint (length + head/tail chars) instead of the raw text.
Deployment & known limitations

output: 'standalone' on Vercel; same artefact runs in any container. No workers, queues, or database. Horizontal scale is bounded by upstream quotas and the analyze route's maxDuration: 60.

  • No persistence. All state lives in memory in the browser session. Refresh resets the flow. Adding auth and persistent essay history is the most natural next step and does not require changes to the analyze pipeline.
  • Pipeline shape, by design and by TODO. Phraseology runs one sentence at a time (parallel within) to keep HF concurrency under the 60s route ceiling; use-analyze-essay and use-resubmit-essay are hand-rolled fetch+useState with in-file TODOs to move to TanStack useMutation.
